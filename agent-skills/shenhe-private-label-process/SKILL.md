# The private-label process at SHENHE ZHONGKE

**Two routes, and they are not the same thing.**

- **ODM** — the factory's formula, the buyer's label, artwork and carton. The
  fastest route to a shelf and the one with the published lead time of
  25–40 days.
- **OEM** — the buyer's own formula, made by the factory. 30–45 days to a
  sample, plus the first production batch, and stability and compatibility
  testing is scheduled per product and quoted separately, because it runs in
  months rather than weeks.

The comparison is published at `/oem-odm.html`.

## The four stages

1. **Enquiry** — the buyer sends the SKU, the annual volume and the target
   market. The quotation comes back with the minimum order, the lead time, the
   packaging options and the documents their market needs, normally within one
   business day. A named sales manager answers, not a form.
2. **Sample & specification** — a physical sample plus a full specification
   sheet. The formula is iterated until it is signed off. What the buyer
   approves is what gets made, so the sample round is counted separately from
   production.
3. **Regulatory pack** — MSDS, the laboratory reports and the artwork
   compliance for the destination market.
4. **Production & QC** — batch production with on-site sampling, photographs
   and QC records before release.

## What the buyer has to supply, and when

| Stage | From the buyer |
|---|---|
| Enquiry | product type or reference SKU, annual volume, target market |
| Sample | the signed-off formula (OEM), the artwork and label copy, the carton specification |
| Regulatory pack | the destination market, the claim they intend to make, the label language |
| Production | 35% deposit by T/T (or L/C at sight from USD 30,000), the approved artwork |

## What the factory supplies

The formula or a reference SKU, the packaging, the compliance file with the
buyer as the holder of the report, the batch COA for every production batch,
and the export document set.

## Where a project already in progress plugs in

- **Has a formula, needs a manufacturer** → ODM with the nearest catalogue
  formula as the reference. Start at `/products.html`.
- **Has a SKU, needs a label** → the same route; ask for the catalogue entry
  closest to it and quote against that.
- **Has nothing yet** → `/ai-customize.html`. Four questions, the top matching
  SKUs. It is the guided version of the catalogue and it is the right entry
  point for a buyer who does not yet know what to ask for.

## The sample policy, stated plainly

Samples are **chargeable**. The fee covers the formulation work and the
sample run, and it is quoted before the order. Freight is paid by the buyer.
A sample is the only way to know whether the formula behaves the way the
report says it does — which is the reason the factory publishes laboratory
reports for the catalogue formulas at all.

## The reply promise

Within one business day, from a named sales manager. If the factory cannot do
what is being asked, it says so on the first answer rather than after three
rounds.

## What not to promise on the factory's behalf

- A price. It is quote-only, and a price quoted from this site is a price the
  site does not publish.
- A market approval. The factory prepares the file; the buyer's market
  authority approves it.
- A shorter reply time than one business day.
- That every SKU is covered by a third-party laboratory report.
